# Kastel World Bible v0.1

## Core dramatic premise

A technologically advanced world has become increasingly legible to integrated platforms and institutions. Identity, finance, communication, work, transport and public administration are heavily mediated by AI-assisted systems.

This order is called the **Blue Rail**.

It is not a single world government or one conspiracy. It is an emergent architecture produced by interoperability, security imperatives, commercial optimisation, convenience, regulation and competition.

Alongside it exists the **Red Rail**: distributed communities and businesses that deliberately preserve independent capabilities while remaining connected to mainstream society.

## The central tension

The recurring question is not:

> Can the heroes destroy the system?

It is:

> Can people remain capable of choosing, building and relating on human terms when convenience, security and coordination increasingly reward integration?

## World tone

- serious underlying systems;
- lively adventure;
- warm community life;
- bureaucratic satire;
- technological improvisation;
- moral ambiguity;
- genuine danger without relentless bleakness;
- recurring humour;
- different cultures treated as cultures, not scenery.

## The Red Rail network

The network consists of semi-independent nodes.

Nodes may include:

- rural houses;
- workshops;
- small farms;
- urban studios;
- trading businesses;
- research spaces;
- clinics;
- maker labs;
- education projects;
- cultural venues.

No node is completely self-sufficient.

Their strength comes from:

- specialised competence;
- mutual trade;
- portability;
- local trust;
- shared knowledge;
- diverse jurisdictions;
- multiple communications and logistics routes.

## The Blue Rail

Blue Rail systems are often genuinely useful.

Characters use them for:

- travel;
- payments;
- healthcare;
- work;
- logistics;
- public identity;
- communication;
- market access.

Drama arises when use becomes dependence, dependence becomes conditionality, and conditionality becomes control.

## Canon rules

1. Technology must obey the project's reality-status system.
2. Geography should remain spatially coherent.
3. Buildings and props persist unless changed in-story.
4. Damage persists until repaired.
5. Characters remember consequences.
6. Travel has time and cost.
7. Money and material constraints matter.
8. Communities require maintenance.
9. Red Rail autonomy is never magical self-sufficiency.
10. Blue Rail actors are not uniformly malicious.
11. Red Rail actors are not uniformly wise.
12. Local cultures and institutions remain consequential.
