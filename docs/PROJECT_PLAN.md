# Project Plan

## 1. Purpose

Kastel World combines speculative fiction with grounded technical and social worldbuilding.

The project has seven linked outputs:

```text
COMIC → WORLD → VISION → FIELD MANUAL → BUILDS → COMMUNITY → REAL PROJECTS
```

The comic creates desire to explore. The world provides depth. The Field Manual translates selected fictional capabilities into present-day reality. Builds make some of those capabilities reproducible. Community experience feeds back into the Commons and, eventually, the fiction.

## 2. Primary design principle

**The story must work without the manual.**

Technology markers are optional doors, not exposition obligations.

A reader should never need to understand a mesh-network protocol to care whether a character gets home before a fire closes the pass.

## 4. Comic mechanics

The comic should use recurring markers sparingly:

- `TECH-###` — real-world technology explanation;
- `NODE-XX-##` — Atlas node;
- `BLUE-###` — Blue Rail institution/system concept;
- `BUILD-###` — reproducible practical project.

Markers can appear in endnotes, hover states, QR links or a discreet panel-side symbol.

## 5. Technical reality classes

Every practical technology is classified as:

- **REAL NOW**
- **EXPERIMENTAL**
- **PROJECTED**
- **FICTIONAL**

Each entry should identify what is available today, what is extrapolated and what remains invented.

## 6. First production target

The first canonical environment is the **South African Garden Node**, inspired by the inland Garden Route / Outeniqua–Klein Karoo transition.

It should demonstrate:

- rural but connected life;
- local AI and private computing;
- resilient internet and communications;
- solar and storage;
- water systems;
- workshop fabrication;
- permaculture/orchard/greenhouse;
- wildfire and flood awareness;
- rotating residency;
- lawful business and trading income;
- practical ties to surrounding towns and services.

## 7. Visual pipeline

The preferred production pipeline is:

```text
open/licensable geography
→ GIS base
→ canonical 3D terrain/site
→ canonical buildings/interiors
→ persistent characters/props
→ saved cameras and story state
→ neutral render
→ comic stylisation
→ lettering
→ cross-link metadata
```

The project should use Google Earth or Street View only as private observational reference where permitted, not as a source to trace or republish. Production assets should come from open/licensable geographic data, owned models, commissioned assets or properly licensed libraries.

## 8. Website

The website should expose three depths:

### Story
Read the comic.

### World
Explore characters, locations, timeline and fictional technology.

### Reality
Explore real technology, builds, sources, limitations and community implementations.

## 9. Community

The community should be organised around making and learning rather than ideological gatekeeping.

Possible areas:

- local AI;
- home lab;
- communications;
- resilient architecture;
- land and permaculture;
- water;
- workshop and fabrication;
- open hardware;
- mapping/GIS;
- build logs;
- comic/worldbuilding.

## 10. Governance rule

The practical layer should favour:

- open standards;
- reversible systems;
- documentation;
- human judgement;
- lawful use;
- non-violence;
- interoperability;
- graceful degradation;
- honest uncertainty.

The fictional layer may depict conflict and coercive institutions, but the real-world project should not become a vehicle for violent, clandestine or offensive operations.
