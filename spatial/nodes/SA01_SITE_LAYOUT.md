# SA-01 Canonical Site Layout

**Site ID:** `SITE-SA-01`  
**Status:** concept v0.1

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the initial physical relationship between the major assets of NODE-SA-01.

It is not a civil-engineering plan.

Its job is to create stable geography for:

- comic continuity;
- 3D modelling;
- movement between buildings;
- environmental storytelling;
- future Atlas views.

## 2. Site concept

The node should feel like a small rural estate rather than a fortified compound.

Core assets:

- House K1;
- Workshop/Lab W1;
- greenhouse/shade house;
- orchard;
- kitchen garden;
- water tanks;
- fire-water reserve;
- energy/utility area;
- vehicle/parking area;
- communications mast or antenna position;
- access road;
- walking path network.

## 4. House/workshop spacing

Initial target:

- workshop approximately 10–20 m from house;
- close enough for everyday use;
- far enough for visual, acoustic and fire separation;
- connected by a clear covered or partly covered path if desired.

## 5. Greenhouse

Place close enough to the house for daily use, but not so close that it dominates the domestic views.

The greenhouse should connect logically to:

- water;
- compost;
- garden beds;
- workshop repair access.

## 6. Water and utility zone

Keep visually discreet but accessible.

Possible assets:

- domestic storage;
- irrigation storage;
- fire-water reserve;
- pumps;
- filters;
- service manifold;
- monitoring sensors.

The fire reserve should be conceptually separate from ordinary irrigation use.

## 7. Productive landscape

The orchard and perennial systems should be spatially integrated rather than treated as decorative background.

Canonical zones may include:

- orchard;
- kitchen garden;
- herbs;
- perennial planting;
- compost;
- nursery/seedling zone;
- dryland/native planting;
- fire-managed landscape.

## 8. Access and movement

The site model should clearly establish:

- main vehicle entrance;
- parking/turning;
- walking route to house;
- workshop service access;
- emergency/service route;
- routes through orchard/garden.

Movement needs to make sense in recurring comic scenes.

## 9. Hazard logic

The first site blockout should consider:

- higher/lower ground;
- drainage;
- wildfire vegetation management;
- wind exposure;
- solar exposure;
- escape/access routes.

These are narrative and design constraints, not claims of engineering sufficiency.

## 10. Spatial storytelling opportunities

The site should provide visually distinct recurring places:

- stoep;
- kitchen table;
- control room;
- workshop apron;
- electronics bench;
- greenhouse aisle;
- water tanks;
- orchard path;
- gate;
- ridge/antenna point.

This allows the node to feel like a real place with its own geography.
