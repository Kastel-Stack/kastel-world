# Kastel Comic — Series Bible v0.1

## Core idea

A recurring community operates across a distributed network of Kastel nodes while the wider world becomes increasingly organised through the Blue Rail.

The community earns money, repairs things, travels, argues, trades, hosts visitors, gets caught in disasters, deals with bureaucracy, solves mysteries and occasionally becomes entangled in much larger systems.

The comic should feel adventurous and intelligent without becoming grim or preachy.

## Tonal reference

The useful *Asterix* comparison is structural rather than stylistic:

- a recognisable home community;
- vivid recurring characters;
- a much larger surrounding order;
- journeys outward from a stable base;
- institutions that can be powerful and absurd;
- community competence as a source of resilience;
- humour mixed with genuine stakes.

Kastel should have its own visual identity.

## What stories are about

Good Kastel stories are usually about at least two layers:

### Surface adventure
A clear immediate problem.

Examples:

- a fire is approaching;
- a courier is late;
- a passport/identity record is wrong;
- a customer shipment is stuck;
- a node has gone silent;
- a workshop machine has failed;
- a visiting official arrives;
- a road has washed out.

### Systems layer
The event reveals something about:

- dependence;
- infrastructure;
- trust;
- technology;
- governance;
- trade;
- ecology;
- institutional power;
- human judgement.

The systems layer should emerge through action, not lecture.

## Recurring story engines

### Blue Rail friction
- scoring or classification errors;
- identity mismatch;
- platform lockout;
- automated bureaucracy;
- conditional access;
- new interoperability rules;
- conflicting institutional objectives.

### Trade and logistics
- moving equipment;
- finding a component;
- crossing jurisdictions;
- keeping a client;
- protecting cashflow;
- negotiating with suppliers.

### Environmental resilience
- wildfire;
- flood;
- drought;
- heat;
- power failure;
- damaged roads.

### Repair and recovery
- rebuilding;
- restoring communications;
- fixing equipment;
- training a replacement;
- relocating a service.

### Exploration
- evaluating a possible new node;
- visiting another culture;
- tracing a missing person or asset;
- recovering knowledge;
- mapping an unfamiliar route.

### Human conflict
- romance;
- jealousy;
- exhaustion;
- generational differences;
- ideological disagreement;
- mistrust;
- ambition;
- comic vanity.

## Violence

Action can occur, but Kastel should not become weapons pornography.

When danger occurs, preferred dramatic tools are:

- escape;
- improvisation;
- rescue;
- evasion;
- environmental hazards;
- negotiation;
- barriers;
- alarms;
- communications;
- coordination.

## Recurring comic rhythm

A typical issue might use:

1. ordinary node life;
2. an interruption;
3. apparently simple problem;
4. widening system context;
5. travel or action;
6. complication;
7. improvisation;
8. human decision;
9. consequence;
10. return to community;
11. small unresolved thread.

## Technology markers

Use sparingly.

A reader should encounter:

> "The field node is dead."

before:

> "TECH-014 explains the LoRa sensor architecture."

Story first. Reference second.
